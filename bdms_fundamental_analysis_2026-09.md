## Verdict

**Fair, with the price leaning on guidance the first half did not meet. At ฿20.20 BDMS discounts
~4–4.5% perpetual free-cash-flow growth at a 7.5–8% cost of equity; management guides 2026
revenue +2–4%, and 1H26 delivered +1% revenue, −7% net profit and 55% occupancy.** Fair band
฿16–23; central ฿18–21 (3.5–4.5% growth, Ke 7.75%). The price sits in the upper half of the band.
The re-rating from 19.4× trailing earnings at Dec-25 to 21× now came on falling earnings, i.e. on a recovery story: July
revenue +8%, occupancy back to 65%, Middle East bookings rebuilding after the March conflict.

What you are buying is the only pan-Thai private network (60 hospitals, 9,345 structured beds,
~48% of listed-sector revenue), AA+ rated, 83% free float, ~14% return on invested capital,
฿30bn of international revenue (more than Bumrungrad's entire top line) and a nine-hospital
Centre-of-Excellence core that earns a 26% EBITDA margin. What you are paying for is growth that
has decelerated every year since 2022 (+23% → +10% → +7% → +3.5% → +1%) while beds kept being
added, and a capital-allocation programme — ฿11bn/yr of hospital capex into 55–65% occupancy, a
฿29bn luxury-residence-and-hotel project on leased land, and a 100% payout financed by ฿8bn of
promissory notes — that is being asked to earn a 4% growth rate it has not shown since 2024.

## What the price implies

Add ฿6.3bn of net debt and ฿4.0bn of minorities to the market cap and the operating business is
priced at ฿331bn, 12.4× EBITDA and ~20× after-tax operating profit. For that to be fair at 8%,
free cash flow must compound at ~4.4% forever from the FY25 level; at 7.5%, ~3.9%. The record:
operating income ฿102.1bn (2023) → 109.4 → 113.2bn → 1H26 +0.9%; net profit ฿14.4bn → 16.0 →
15.8bn (adjusted ฿16.2bn) → 1H26 −6.8%. EBITDA margin 24.2% → 24.3% → 23.9% → 23.3% (1H26), with
4Q25 carrying ฿416M of one-offs (Hat Yai flood, lifelong-medical-care provision, asset write-offs).
Since 2023, earnings have compounded at ~3%/yr, and the second derivative is negative.

To call ฿20.20 cheap you need the July-2026 run-rate (+8% revenue, 65% occupancy) to hold through
2H26 and the 2027 bed additions (~9,400 structured) to fill at Centre-of-Excellence margins — that
gets you to ~฿17bn of profit in 2027 and a ~฿24–25 stock at today's multiple, which is where the
sell side sits (labelled: 23 analysts, average target ฿24.8). To call it expensive you need the
1H26 pattern — flat Thai volumes, insurance growth +2%, occupancy falling into added capacity — to
be the new normal, in which case 3% growth and Ke 8% give ฿14.5. The middle is priced.

## Operating picture — a network adding beds into flat census

**Volume is flat; price and complexity carry the top line.** Outpatient visits per day 32,987
(2023) → 34,849 → 35,685 (2025) → 35,979 (2Q26); average daily census 4,509 → 4,489 → 4,321 →
3,654 (2Q26, −10% YoY); available beds 6,577 → 6,664 → 6,722 → 6,639; occupancy 68.6% → 67.4% →
64.3% → 55.0% in 2Q26 (59.5% in 1Q26, 65% in July). Average stay 2.7 days, unchanged. Management's
own explanation for 2025 growth is "higher price intensity from complex cases" while inpatient
volumes fell on the economic slowdown. Derived revenue per inpatient day ~฿34,500, per outpatient
visit ~฿4,100. Specialty growth in 2025: trauma +12%, cancer +11%, orthopaedics +10%,
cardiovascular +9%.

**International is 28% of hospital revenue (~฿30bn) and diversified, which is the structural
advantage over BH.** Thai/international 72/28 in 2025 (31/69 at 1Q26 seasonally). Top nationalities
by 2025 growth: USA +19%, Myanmar +31%, Qatar +36%, UK +14%, Germany +16%; China −3%, Cambodia
−42%. Middle East is only ~4% of group revenue (Kuwait was 0.7% before the referral suspension, vs
6% at BH), so the March-2026 conflict cost −24% on a small base (−13% in 1H26) and is already
rebuilding (3,500+ August bookings). Cambodia fell from ~3% to ~2% of revenue (−73% in 1H26) on the
border closure. The July print, +14% international ex-Cambodia and Middle East, is the recovery
case in one number.

**Payer mix is the co-payment exposure.** Self-pay 47.7%, insurance 37.5% (local 32%, foreign
6%), corporate 8%, Social Security 3%, others 3.5%. The OIC co-payment rule (new individual health
policies from 20-Mar-2025; 30–50% co-pay triggered by frequent simple-disease claims) hits BDMS
more than any peer because insurance is 37–39% of revenue and management wants 40–45%; 2026 is
the first full renewal cycle. Insurance revenue +5% (2025) → +2% (1H26) → +11% (July). The
company's answer is a Pricing Intelligence Unit, utilisation-review AI and value-based packages —
i.e. it is defending price, which is where the whole sector's growth has come from.

**Receivables: the Middle East pattern is showing here too, at a smaller scale.** Collection
period 41 days and unchanged, but the ageing note moved: 6–12 months past due ฿545M (Dec-25) →
฿1,327M (Jun-26); over 12 months ฿459M → ฿694M; ECL allowance ฿473M → ฿602M. ฿2.0bn is now more
than six months old, 16% of gross receivables, against ฿1.0bn six months earlier. Provisioning is
better than BH's (allowance 30% of the >6-month book vs BH's 8%) and the numbers are 2% of revenue,
not 25%. Read the note each half-year; it is a sector signal, not yet a BDMS problem.

**Margins: a two-tier network.** Nine Centre-of-Excellence hospitals (Bangkok HQ 16% of revenue,
Samitivej Sukhumvit/Srinakarin 13%, Bangkok Pattaya 5%, Phyathai 2, Bangkok Phuket, others) are
51% of revenue and 54% of hospital EBITDA at a ~26% margin; the other ~50 hospitals run ~22%.
Group EBITDA margin guidance ~24%; 1H26 23.3%, 2Q26 22.0% (the trough quarter). Doctor fees
(21% of revenue, +5.6% in 2025) and staff (24%, +2.8%) grow faster than the +3.5% top line; D&A
+12% in 2025 on the four 2025 openings (Samitivej Children's, Chiangmai, Phyathai Bowin, Khao Yai).

**Capital allocation is the question the multiple does not ask.** Hospital capex ฿8.3bn (2023) →
11.7 → 11.3bn (2025), guided at 8–10% of revenue for 2026–28, into a network running 55–65%
occupancy; ~9,400 structured beds by 2027; a private proton-therapy centre (2029) and a
rehabilitation building at HQ. On top sits WellEra: 262 luxury residences, a 168-key hotel, a
wellness clinic and retail on 13 rai leased from the Privy Purse Bureau opposite Lumphini Park,
budget raised in June 2026 from ฿23.5bn to ฿29.0bn (9% of market cap), completion 2030, ~฿26bn
still to spend, funded "by internal cash and debt", and described by management as adding ~1% to
the 2030–34 revenue growth rate. That is a real-estate development inside a hospital company; the
฿893M of "real estate development for sale" already on the balance sheet is the first instalment.
Meanwhile FY25's ฿15.9bn dividend (100% payout, ฿4bn of it special) took the group from ฿2.1bn net
cash in March to ฿6.3bn net debt in June, with ฿8bn of promissory notes outstanding. Net
debt/EBITDA 0.6× is nothing for an AA+ credit; the point is direction. Free cash flow after all
capex will run ~฿7–10bn/yr against ~฿12–13bn of dividends through 2030, so net debt trends toward
฿25–30bn (~1× EBITDA) by the time WellEra opens.

## Capital return

Policy ≥50% of consolidated profit; actual 75–77% in FY22–24, 100% in FY25 including the ฿0.25
special (labelled "a one-time distribution carried out under strict financial discipline"). FY26
interim ฿0.35, flat. Regular run-rate ฿0.75–0.80 (labelled consensus ฿0.82 for 2026) → 3.7–4.1%
yield. Hold-return arithmetic at a frozen multiple: yield ~3.9% + growth ~3–4% ≈ **7–8%/yr in THB**
before the gross-up credit. No buyback programme exists or has existed in 2024–26 (verified: no
treasury-stock line, no SET announcement); the share count has been 15,892M throughout. BDMS holds
no stake in Bumrungrad (the 22.7% was sold in Nov–Dec 2020).

## Sensitivity map — what moves the value

- **Long-run growth.** Each +0.5% adds ~10% (฿18.3 → ฿20.2 → ฿22.4 at 4 / 4.5 / 5%, Ke 8%). The
  evidence for ≥4%: July +8%, international +10% ex-Cambodia/Middle East, ~700 beds coming. The
  evidence against: +1% in 1H26, insurance +2%, 2–4% guidance.
- **Cost of equity.** ±0.5% ≈ ∓10% (฿18.3 → ฿16.2 at 8.5%, g 4%). The stock's size and
  liquidity are why the market's Ke is closer to 7.5% than 8.5%.
- **Occupancy.** 1 point ≈ 66 beds of census ≈ 24,000 inpatient-days ≈ ฿0.8bn revenue ≈ ฿0.3bn
  EBITDA ≈ 1.5% of earnings. 2Q26 ran 10 points below 2Q24.
- **Insurance co-payment.** Insurance is 37–39% of revenue; simple-disease claims ~5–20% of
  that. A 10% cut in the affected slice ≈ 1–2% of revenue ≈ 3–5% of earnings.
- **WellEra.** ฿26bn to spend for ~1% of revenue CAGR; if the residences sell at cost the project
  is a 0% ROIC on 8% of market cap ≈ −8% of value versus redeploying at the group's 14%.
- **Margin.** 1 point of EBITDA margin ≈ ฿1.1bn ≈ ฿0.9bn net ≈ 6% of earnings; the 2Q26 trough was
  2.6 points below 2Q24.
- **Middle East / Cambodia.** ~6% of revenue combined; both externally switched; recovery is
  upside already partly in the July number.

## Relation to the register

Candidate, not urgent. Of the five hospital names it is the one to own for the sector's structure
(network, insurance relationships, international diversification, credit), and the one where the
price already assumes the recovery. Two observable tests before acting: (1) 3Q26 results
(Nov-2026) — hospital revenue growth ≥5% and occupancy ≥63% confirm July was a trend; (2) the
FY26 dividend (Feb-2027) — a regular ≥฿0.80 with no special, and a stated capex plan that keeps
net debt/EBITDA under 1× through WellEra. A print below ฿17 (Ke 8%, g ~3.5%) is where the growth
assumption stops needing to be right.

## Terms (for cold reads)

- **Operating income:** BDMS's term for hospital revenue + sales of goods + other income
  excluding dividend income; EBITDA margins are quoted on it.
- **Structured vs available beds:** structured = maximum physical capacity (9,345); available =
  staffed and licensed beds in service (~6,700); occupancy is on available beds.
- **Average daily census (ADC):** inpatients in beds on an average day.
- **Centres of Excellence (CoE):** the nine flagship hospitals that take the network's complex
  and international cases.
- **Co-payment rule:** OIC requirement from 20-Mar-2025 that new individual health policies
  carry 30–50% co-pay after frequent simple-disease claims.
- **WellEra:** the ฿29bn mixed-use residence/hotel/clinic development on leased Langsuan land,
  due 2030.
- **BOI-promoted profit:** profit exempt from corporate tax under Board of Investment
  certificates; dividends paid from it carry no tax credit and no withholding.
- **ROIC / implied growth:** as in the BH note; invested capital here includes lease liabilities
  and the land-revaluation surplus.
