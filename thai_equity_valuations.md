## TLI — embedded value (updated 12-Jun-2026)

**Verdict: trades at roughly two-thirds of conservative worth.** At ฿11.30 the stock sells for
~0.67× a worth of ~฿16.90/share counting only policies already written and the investments
behind them — ฿19–22 giving credit for future sales — while paying ฿0.60/yr (5.3% cash, plus
the gross-up credit). The worth figure moves with Thai rates; date it wherever quoted.

### Anchors at ฿11.30 (11-Jun-26 close; 11,450M shares; market cap ฿129.4bn)

| Anchor | Value |
|---|---|
| Embedded value, Dec-25 (Milliman) | ฿202.8bn = ฿17.71/sh |
| **EV marked to 10y ~2.28%** (+62bp × −3.7%/50bp gearing) | **≈ ฿193.5bn = ฿16.90/sh → P/EV ~0.67×** |
| **Allocated comprehensive equity, Mar-26** (equity ex-OCI + net CSM) | **฿193.5bn = ฿16.90/sh** (+2.2% QoQ) |
| Book equity, Mar-26 (volatile — do not anchor on it) | ฿127.3bn = ฿11.12/sh (P/B 1.02×) |
| Net worth alone (ANW, Dec-25) | ฿13.94/sh → price = 0.81× |
| FY25 profit ฿11.88bn → EPS ฿1.04; TTM EPS ฿1.09 | P/E ~10.4× |
| Dividend FY25 ฿0.60 (payout 57.9%) | 5.3% + gross-up credit |
| Appraisal value (marked EV + 5–10× run-rate VONB ~฿5.5–6bn, soft) | ≈ ฿19–22/sh |
| Justified P/EV 0.8–1.5× on marked EV | ฿13.5–25 (bear-fair ฿13.5–17) |

The two intrinsic anchors — rate-marked EV and allocated comprehensive equity — converge at
~฿16.9 by different roads (one marks the rate hit, one strips it but uses locked-in contract
economics). Partly coincidence, still reassuring.

### What the price implies

Market cap (฿129.4bn) sits **below the Dec-25 adjusted net worth alone (฿159.7bn)**: the buyer
is paid ~฿30bn (−฿2.64/sh) to take the ฿43.1bn of in-force value plus every future policy
sold. Even stripping the ฿9.3bn land/property revaluation inside net worth, the price is still
0.86× the residual. To call ฿11.30 fair you need net worth materially overstated AND zero
ongoing franchise — or a permanent ~33% control/float discount.

### Q1-2026 — the rate exposure realized

The Thai 10y rose 1.66% → 2.21% in-quarter (≈2.28% by 9-Jun). Unrealized losses on bonds drove
other-comprehensive-income −฿31.0bn; book equity fell 17.6% to ฿11.12/sh — which is exactly
SET's printed P/BV of 0.98 at ฿10.90. Capital ratio (CAR) fell 555.9% → 463.3%, still 3.3× the
140% regulatory floor. Note the company's own ±50bp equity sensitivity is only ∓฿14bn — the
excess in the actual move is curve-shape, credit-spread, and reclassification noise. **Rule:
while book equity is the volatile line, value the company on EV / allocated comprehensive
equity, not price-to-book.**

### Operating picture — claims vs new business

Q1-26 net profit +21.8% (core +18.1%). The claims side improved: operating variance swung
−฿170M → +฿197M net of tax, driven by lower medical claims — the Mar-2025 copayment rules
protecting the in-force book, where health riders are ≈43% of in-force value. The cost shows
up in new business: rider sales −61%, total new-business volume (APE) −29%, value of new
business −45% to ฿1.40bn, margin 53.3%. That margin was struck on the low 1.66% opening
curve; cohorts written from Q2 onward price on ~2.2–2.3% curves and recover roughly +20%
mechanically (new-business value is rate-POSITIVE, +19.4% per +0.5%). FY26 new-business value
run-rate ~฿5–6bn (soft estimate — distorted Q1 base, rate recovery, seasonality).

The trade is EV-arithmetic-favorable: in-force value ≈ 6× a year of new-business value, so
protecting in-force claims experience at the cost of slower rider sales adds value near-term.

### Dividend, capital, hold-return

FY25 dividend ฿0.60 (+20% YoY; payout 57.9%; ฿0.50 in FY24, ฿0.34+฿0.16 in FY23). Covered
~1.8× by TTM earnings; payout is OIC-regulated but nowhere near capital-constrained at 463%
CAR. Hold-return arithmetic at a frozen multiple: ~8.9% operating EV growth, less ~3.5% of EV
paid out, plus 5.3% cash ≈ **~10–11%/yr in THB before any re-rating** — conditional on the
operating assumptions holding.

### Sensitivity map — what moves the embedded value
*(structural numbers from the Dec-25 Milliman report; refresh when the next report appears
~Feb-2027, not with price pulls)*

- **Interest rates — the dominant lever, and it points two ways at once.** On paper, EV falls
  3.7% for every +0.5% in Thai yields and rises 5.1% per −0.5% (about −฿0.66 / +฿0.89 per
  share); this sensitivity nearly tripled during 2025. The real business runs the opposite
  way: higher rates make new policies more profitable (+19.4% per +0.5%) and improve what
  maturing money gets reinvested at. Q1-26's rate rise cut the paper value while making the
  underlying business healthier. Rule: treat rate-driven EV moves as accounting weather, not
  business change — FY25's +12.2% EV growth was the same weather blowing the other way
  (฿11.9bn of it was bond prices rising as rates fell). At +1.0–1.5% from the Dec-25 curve,
  EV ≈ ฿15.7–16.4/sh.
- **Medical-claims assumptions — the biggest non-market lever.** Health riders are ≈43% of the
  value of policies already on the books. If claims run permanently 10% worse than assumed,
  EV drops 4.0% (≈฿8.0bn ≈ ฿0.70/share — roughly half a year of the ~9% the business adds
  annually from operations); 10% better adds the same back. The load-bearing assumption is
  that claims stay controlled through repricing and the 2025 copayment rules — the one risk
  Milliman singled out in words rather than numbers. Q1-26 claims came in better than
  assumed: first confirming evidence, one mild quarter, flag stays at half-mast.
- **The discount rate the company picks for itself (8.0%).** Future profits are valued at a
  rate Thai Life chooses, set 5 points above an assumed 3.0% long-run government yield;
  moving it ±1 point changes EV −2.9% / +3.3%. Second-order at this price: the market already
  values the entire future-profit block at roughly zero, so the disagreement isn't about
  which discount rate to apply.
- **The assumed climb back to 3.0% rates — the sensitivity the report never publishes.** All
  projections assume today's ~2.3% yields drift up to 3.0% over time. If Thai rates instead
  stay low for good, future investment income shrinks with no offsetting one-time gain
  anywhere — the quiet bear case the published table doesn't show. (The 2025 trim of that
  target from 3.2% to 3.0% cost ฿0.48bn even after a partly offsetting change; the standalone
  cost is larger and undisclosed.) Everything else published is minor: stock prices ±10% → EV
  ±3.1%; expenses ±10% → ∓0.8%; policy-lapse rates ±10% → ∓0.1%.

### Why the discount persists, and caveats

Free float ~20.4%; Chaiyawan family + group ~65%, Meiji Yasuda 17%. No buyback lever, no
catalyst path — 0.6–0.7× can be a steady state; the dividend is the only contractual return
route. Caveats: the claims improvement is one seasonally-mild quarter on a young copayment
book; the FY26 new-business run-rate is an estimate, not guidance; the worth figure itself
moves with the curve (฿17.71 on Dec-25 rates, ฿16.90 on June's).

### Relation to the trim

This analysis informs the DISSENT record only. The TLI reduction is weight/legacy-driven and
proceeds regardless — sub-intrinsic cheapness does not suspend, pause, or resize the glide.

**The growth question — UNRESOLVED:** at ~95–100% payout,

**Plain-English summary:** think of ADVANC as a machine that pays you ~฿17 per share in

answer most: that long-run growth assumption, and Thai government bond yields.

## Terms (for cold reads)

- **Embedded value (EV):** an insurer's net worth plus the present value of profits expected
  from policies already sold. Excludes future sales.
- **ANW / VIF:** the two halves of EV — adjusted net worth (assets minus liabilities,
  regulator's basis) and value of in-force (those future profits).
- **VONB / APE:** value of new business = profit expected from this period's new policies;
  APE = a standardized measure of new-policy sales volume.
- **CSM / allocated comprehensive equity:** the accounting (IFRS 17) cousins — CSM is unearned
  future profit on the balance sheet; allocated comprehensive equity = equity excluding
  bond-mark swings, plus CSM net of tax/reinsurance. Updated quarterly.
- **CAR:** regulatory capital held vs required (floor 140%).
- **SOTP:** sum-of-the-parts — valuing a parent by adding up its stakes.
- **DDM:** dividend-discount model — a stock is worth its future dividends, discounted; fair
  value ≈ next-year dividend ÷ (required return − growth).
- **Gross-up credit:** Thai dividend tax credit for individuals on dividends paid from
  corporate-taxed profits; raises the effective yield for a Thai filer.

## Addendum 28-Sep-2026 — BH candidate and the dividend gross-up comparison

*Appended after the June set; the June sections above are unchanged. Full workings in

**BH (Bumrungrad) — verdict: fair to modestly rich.** Trailing P/E 20.7× basic / 22.5× diluted,
EV/EBITDA 14.2× on ฿10.4bn net cash, FCF yield 3.9%, ROIC ~52%. The price discounts ~3.5–4%
perpetual FCF growth; the record is three flat revenue years (฿25.5 / 25.9 / 25.4bn), falling
volumes (bed-days −4% in 2025 after −7.5%), Thai revenue −3%/yr, and a record 40% EBITDA margin.
All growth is non-Thai and Gulf-led (Middle East 23% of revenue, +13.5% in 1H26) and is showing up
as receivables (collection period 60 → 78 days). Fair band ฿145–210, central ฿170–190. Phuket
(฿4.3bn, 120 beds, 2H27) drags 2027–29. Convertibles add 9% to the share count by Aug-2027.
Watch items: FY26 final dividend ≥฿5 (payout thesis) and DSO back under 65 days. Candidate only.

**Gross-up play — is BH a good vehicle vs PTT and TLI?** Credit eligibility from each company's
own notices: BH ~100% (interim ฿4.00 = ฿3.998 non-BOI + ฿0.002 BOI; BOI benefit ฿10M in 2025);
TLI ~99% (20% CIT, 19% effective; SET form split not retrieved); PTT ~63% (FY25 ฿2.30 = ฿1.45
credit + ฿0.82 PTTEP-sourced at 50% petroleum tax, no credit + ฿0.03 BOI). A credit-eligible baht
is worth 25% more than the withheld amount to a filer at a 10% marginal rate; a no-credit baht is
worth nothing extra, and is penalised above the 10% bracket.

| Net yield, electing, at marginal 10% / 20% | Cash yield | |
|---|---|---|
| TLI (฿0.60) | 5.26% | **5.91% / 5.25%** |
| BH (฿9, full-payout case) | 4.56% | **5.13% / 4.56%** |
| PTT (฿2.10) | 4.88% | 5.09% / 4.54% |
| BH (฿6, old payout) | 3.04% | 3.42% / 3.04% |

**Ranking: TLI first; BH second only if the doubled interim signals a ~฿9 regular run-rate,
otherwise third.** BH's credit quality equals TLI's and beats PTT's, but its regular yield is the
lowest of the three unless the payout stays near 100%. BH specials are fully credit-eligible

policy. PTT's PTTEP-sourced third is the only structurally no-credit line among the three and
argues against PTT in an electing account above the 10% bracket.
