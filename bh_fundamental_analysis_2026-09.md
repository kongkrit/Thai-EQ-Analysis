## Verdict

**Fair to modestly rich. At ฿197.50 the price discounts ~3.5–4% perpetual growth in free cash
flow from a business that has printed flat revenue for three years, falling patient volumes, and
a record 40% EBITDA margin.** Fair band ฿145–210; central ฿170–190 (3% growth, 8% cost of
equity). The +26% move since the Dec-25 close (฿157) came on +2% earnings, i.e. it was almost
all re-rating from 16.7× to ~21× trailing P/E.

What you are buying is real: a debt-free, ~50%-ROIC franchise with two-thirds of revenue from
non-Thai patients, ฿10.4bn of net cash, and a board that has just doubled the interim dividend.
What you are paying for is growth the operating numbers do not yet show. The decision hinges on
one question: does Middle East revenue intensity (+28% in 1H26) keep offsetting Thai attrition
(−3%/yr) and volume decline, or is the current price/mix run the top of the margin cycle?

## What the price implies

Strip ฿10.4bn of net cash from the ฿157bn market cap and the operating business is priced at
฿146.7bn, 14.2× EBITDA and ~19× after-tax operating profit. For that to be fair at an 8% cost
of equity, free cash flow has to compound at ~3.7% forever. The record says: revenue ฿25.5bn
(2023), ฿25.9bn (2024), ฿25.4bn (2025), ฿12.5bn in 1H26 (+1.8%). Net profit ฿7.0bn → ฿7.8bn →
฿7.5bn → 1H26 +2.4%. The compounding since 2023 has been roughly 1–2%/yr in earnings, with
BOI tax breaks (฿147M in 2024, ฿10M in 2025) now gone.

To call ฿197.50 cheap you need either (a) Middle East flows to keep growing double-digit and
Kuwait to come back, or (b) a permanent payout step-up to ~100% so the cash stops piling up at
2% yields. To call it expensive you need Thai attrition to continue and the margin to mean-revert
toward 37–38%. My read: neither tail is priced; the middle is.

## Operating picture — a price/mix business, not a volume business

**Volumes are falling.** Beds in operation 515 (538 in 2024, 537 in 2023); occupied bed-days
−4.2% in 2025 after −7.5% in 2024; outpatients per day 2,649 vs 2,913 in 2023 (−9%). Operational
bed utilisation 68%, outpatient utilisation 48% against a 5,500/day capacity — no constraint
anywhere. Revenue per occupied bed-day rose ~2.3% in 2025, and management's "revenue intensity"
metric ran +6.6% in 1H26 (+28% for Middle East patients). The whole top line is being held up by
case complexity and pricing.

**Thai patients (34% of revenue) are leaving.** −1.8% in 2025, −2.9% in 1H26, Thai *volume*
−6.5% in 2Q26. This is the quiet structural line: the domestic upper-middle class is either
priced out or going to BDMS's network. Management offsets with oncology and cardiology intensity
(+2.1% Thai intensity in 2025).

**Non-Thai (66%) is the whole growth engine and it is Gulf-concentrated.** Middle East 23% of
net patient revenue; Qatar is now the single largest nationality. 1H26 non-Thai +5.6%, or +10.2%
excluding Cambodia (which collapsed −47% in 2025 and −79% in 4Q25 after the border conflict).
The 2025 revenue shortfall of ฿500M came from four sources: Cambodia, China, Kuwait, Thailand.
Kuwait's government referral cut is the known hole; its return is the upside catalyst the sell
side keeps citing, and I have no way to size it from the filings.

**The Gulf growth is showing up as receivables.** Collection period 60.5 days (Dec-25) → 71.7
(Mar-26) → 78.1 (Jun-26), "mostly due to ... certain Middle East accounts". Trade receivables
jumped ฿1.9bn in 1H26 to ฿6.2bn; the 91–180-day bucket doubled to ฿869M during 2025; ECL
provision doubled to ฿63M; allowance ฿201M against ฿1.14bn more than 90 days past due. Government
third-party payors are 22% of revenue. These are sovereign payors that have historically paid,
slowly — but 1H26 operating cash flow was ฿2.5bn against ฿3.7bn net profit because of it.
**Rule: track DSO every quarter; if it passes the 90-day policy ceiling, the Middle East growth is
being financed by BH's balance sheet.**

**Margin is at a record and costs are rising underneath.** EBITDA margin 37.5% (2023) → 39.6%
→ 39.9% → 40.3% (1H26), achieved by cost of operations falling faster than revenue. But 2Q26
cost of hospital operations +4.4% on +3.8% revenue ("physicians' fees and supply costs"), selling
expenses +12.7%. The easy cost wins look taken.

**Phuket is the growth capex and the near-term earnings drag.** ฿4.3bn project, 120 beds
opening 2H27, 92 more in years 6–10; ~฿1.75bn paid into the subsidiary so far, ฿850M uncalled.
PP&E rose ฿862M in 1H26. Expect 2–4 years of start-up losses from 2H27 (a ฿200–400M/yr pretax
drag is the normal range for a greenfield private hospital), plus D&A step-up. The 2026–30 plan
also has Bangkok infrastructure spend. Free cash flow will run below the FY25 ฿6.2bn for 2026–27.

## Capital return — the one thing that has changed

FY24 ฿5.00 regular (51% payout). FY25 ฿5.00 regular + ฿6.00 special = 116% payout, funded from
the ฿16.5bn cash pile. FY26 interim ฿4.00 paid 10-Sep-2026, double last year's ฿2.00. If the
board is moving to a ~100% regular payout, D1 ≈ ฿9–9.5 → ~4.8% yield, and the hold-return
arithmetic becomes yield ~4.8% + growth ~2–3% ≈ **7–8%/yr in THB at a frozen multiple**, before
gross-up credit. If it reverts to ~55%, yield is ~3% and the rest accumulates as cash earning ~2%
— the value-trap version. The interim doubling is the strongest single piece of evidence for the
former, and the Phuket funding need (~฿2.5bn remaining) is small against ฿10bn of net cash and
฿8bn/yr of operating cash flow.

## Dividend-tax gross-up: BH vs PTT vs TLI (prices 25-Sep-2026)

**Verdict: BH's dividends are as credit-clean as TLI's and cleaner than PTT's, but BH only becomes
a competitive gross-up vehicle if the payout step-up sticks. Ranking on credit-adjusted yield:
TLI first; BH second if the regular dividend is ~฿9, third behind PTT if it reverts to ~฿6.**

**How the credit works (Section 47 bis).** A Thai-resident individual may elect to include
dividends in personal income, gross them up by the corporate tax already paid, and claim that tax
as a credit. On profit taxed at 20% the credit is 20/80 = 25% of the cash dividend, on top of the
10% withholding refunded. The election is all-or-nothing across every Thai dividend received in
the year. Three kinds of dividend therefore behave differently per ฿100 of cash:

| Source of the dividend | Value electing, at marginal rate 10% / 20% / 25% | Keep 10% WHT as final |
|---|---|---|
| Profit taxed at 20% CIT (credit) | 112.5 / 100.0 / 93.8 | 90 |
| Profit taxed outside the Revenue Code (no credit; PTT's PTTEP-sourced part) | 90.0 / 80.0 / 75.0 | 90 |
| BOI-exempt profit (excluded from income, no WHT) | 100 / 100 / 100 | 100 |

Break-even marginal rate for a credit dividend is 28%; for a no-credit dividend it is 10%. So the
play is worth most to a filer whose grossed-up income stays inside the 0–25% brackets, and every
no-credit baht in the basket erodes the election.

**What each company's own announcements say about its dividend source:**

- **BH:** interim of 10-Sep-2026 ฿4.00 = ฿3.998 from non-BOI profit + ฿0.002 from BOI profit
  (SET notice 14-Aug-2026). FY25 final ฿9 (incl. ฿6 special) was "payable out of net profits and
  retained earnings taxable at the applicable rates". Effective tax rate 18.8%; BOI benefit ฿10M
  in 2025. **~100% credit-eligible, specials included.**
- **PTT:** FY25 ฿2.30 = ฿1.45 from 20%-taxed profit (credit) + ฿0.82 from PTTEP dividends taxed
  at 50% petroleum income tax (no credit) + ฿0.03 BOI (exempt). FY24 interim had the same 64/36
  split. **Only ~63% credit-eligible, and the 36% is structurally recurring** because PTTEP is the
  largest earnings contributor.
- **TLI:** life-insurance profit taxed under the Revenue Code at 20%; FY25 effective rate 19%
  (non-taxable income ฿110M of ฿14.7bn pretax). **~99% credit-eligible.** Caveat: TLI's SET
  dividend form with the source split could not be retrieved (site viewer), so this is inferred
  from the tax note, not read off the notice.

**Credit-adjusted yield at 25-Sep-2026 prices** (BH ฿197.50, PTT ฿43.00, TLI ฿11.40):

| | Dividend basis | Cash yield | Net yield electing, marginal 10% / 20% / 25% | Net yield keeping WHT | Uplift from electing at 10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TLI** | FY25 regular ฿0.60 | 5.26% | **5.91% / 5.25% / 4.92%** | 4.74% | +25% |
| **PTT** | FY25 regular ฿2.10 | 4.88% | 5.09% / 4.54% / 4.26% | 4.40% | +16% |
| **BH** | ฿9 (interim ฿4 + final ฿5, full-payout case) | 4.56% | **5.13% / 4.56% / 4.27%** | 4.10% | +25% |
| **BH** | ฿6 (old ~55% payout) | 3.04% | 3.42% / 3.04% / 2.85% | 2.73% | +25% |
| BH, trailing 12m cash incl. special | ฿13 | 6.58% | 7.40% / 6.58% / 6.17% | 5.92% | +25% |

**Reading it.** The credit quality ranking is BH ≈ TLI > PTT: a baht of BH or TLI dividend is worth
25% more to a low-bracket filer than the withheld amount, a baht of PTT only 16% more, and at a 20%
marginal rate PTT's uplift is ~3% because the PTTEP-sourced third is taxed with no credit. The yield
ranking is what decides it: TLI's 5.3% cash beats both, and BH's regular yield only matches PTT's
if the board keeps paying ~100% of earnings. BH's specials are fully credit-eligible, so in a year
like FY25 (฿13 trailing cash) it is the best of the three on this metric, but specials are not a
policy. Sizing rule: for the gross-up sleeve, BH belongs behind TLI, and ahead of PTT only once
a ≥฿5 final for FY26 confirms the ฿9 run-rate. PTT's no-credit portion is the one line that argues
against holding it in an electing account at all above the 10% bracket.

## Sensitivity map — what moves the value

- **Long-run growth is the whole argument.** Each +0.5% of perpetual growth adds ~11% to fair
  value (฿171 → ฿189 → ฿211 at 3 / 3.5 / 4%). The evidence for ≥3.5% is 1H26 non-Thai
  ex-Cambodia +10% and Middle East intensity; the evidence against is three flat years and
  falling volumes.
- **Thai 10-year / cost of equity.** +0.5% on Ke knocks ~9% off (฿171 → ฿157). Beta is 0.18 on
  the tape, which is why the market's Ke is probably nearer 7.5% than 8.5% — that alone gets
  you to ฿189 at 3% growth.
- **Middle East payors.** 23% of revenue and essentially all the growth. Two risks: policy
  (Kuwait showed a government can switch referrals off in a year) and collection (DSO now 78
  days). One upside: Kuwait switching back on.
- **Margin.** Every 1 point of EBITDA margin ≈ ฿250M EBITDA ≈ ฿200M net ≈ 2.7% of earnings.
  A reversion from 40% to 38% costs ~5% of value.
- **Phuket ramp.** ฿300M/yr pretax drag from 2H27 ≈ 3% of earnings for 2–3 years; not a
  valuation driver, but it will make the 2027–28 prints look worse than the franchise.
- **Dilution, Aug-2027.** Convertibles add 9% to the share count; already in diluted EPS.
  Fair values above on the diluted basis are ~8% lower.

## Ownership and governance

Bangkok Bank group is the anchor: Bangkok Insurance 9.95%, Bangkok Bank 6.69%, Sophonpanich Co
3.49%, Chai Sophonpanich (chairman, 82) 1.44% → ~21.6%. BDMS no longer appears in the top ten.
Thai NVDR 9.7%, foreign 32.3% against a 49% limit, free float 73%. Social Security Office 3.3%,
Vayupak 2.4%. No controller extracting value; no buyback history; the dividend is the return
route, and the family-bank group has just shown it wants the cash out.

## Caveats

The FCF anchor (฿6.3bn) is FY25 actual; 2026–27 will print lower on Phuket capex and the
receivables build, so a mechanical FCF-yield screen will make BH look worse before it looks
better. The peer table is trailing SET multiples on one day. Kuwait and the Q3 guidance were
given verbally at the analyst meeting and are not in any document I could read. No consensus
estimates were used.

## Relation to the register

Candidate only. Nothing here argues for urgency: the franchise is not going anywhere, the

FY26 dividend announcement (Feb-2027): a final of ≥฿5 on top of the ฿4 interim confirms the

## Terms (for cold reads)

- **Revenue intensity:** management's term for revenue per patient episode; growth here means
  more complex cases or higher prices, not more patients.
- **DSO / collection period:** days of revenue sitting in receivables; the policy ceiling is 90.
- **ROIC:** after-tax operating profit ÷ (equity − net cash); the return on money actually
  tied up in the hospital, as opposed to ROE which is diluted by idle cash.
- **BOI benefit:** Board of Investment tax deduction (200% of qualifying community-health
  spend in 2024, cut to 120% in 2025); a one-off ฿147M → ฿10M swing, not operating.
- **Implied growth:** the perpetual growth rate that makes FCF ÷ (Ke − g) + net cash equal the
  current market cap.
