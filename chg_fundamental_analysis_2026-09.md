## Verdict

**Fair. At ฿1.58 the price discounts ~2–2.5% perpetual free-cash-flow growth from a mid-tier
provincial hospital chain whose earnings have fallen three years running (฿1,046M → 965 → 929M)
and whose beds are emptier every quarter (occupancy 71% → 67% → 64% → 54% in 2Q26).** Fair band
฿1.35–2.10; central ฿1.50–1.75 (2–3% growth, 9% cost of equity). The price is in the lower half of
the band, which is where it belongs: a 6.4% free-cash-flow yield and a 4.4% fully credit-eligible
dividend are paying you to wait, but what you are waiting for is a ฿2bn bed-expansion cycle
(938 → 1,700+ beds by 2030) that starts with start-up losses in 2027, not with growth.

What you are buying is real: a net-cash, ~14%-ROIC operator with a 40-year franchise on the
eastern seaboard, 565,000 Social Security registrants that walk in the door regardless of the
economy, and a controlling family that has paid ฿770M every year since 2023 through falling
profits. What you are paying for is the 2027–28 capacity step-up filling at cash-patient prices.
The decision hinges on two things that are observable before you need to act: whether the
Social Security Office's October-2026 rate review restores what the 2024–25 reimbursement cuts
took away, and whether general-patient inpatient volumes (−14% in 2Q26) stop falling.

## What the price implies

Strip ฿0.8bn of net cash and ฿0.4bn of minorities and the operating business is priced at ฿17.0bn,
9.5× EBITDA and ~17× after-tax operating profit. At a 9% cost of equity that is fair if free cash
flow compounds at ~2.3% forever. The record: hospital revenue ฿7.73bn (2023) → 8.24 → 8.35bn →
1H26 +3.2%; net profit ฿1,046M → 965 → 929M → 1H26 +10% (+1% ex the Arincare gain, per the
company's own normalisation). Earnings have compounded at −6%/yr since 2023, although half of that
is the loss of the Pattaya City Hospital management contract (other income −฿173M in 2025) and the
2024–25 Social Security write-downs (฿53M and ~฿103M), neither of which recurs.

To call ฿1.58 cheap you need the 2027 beds (Rayong 200, CH3 +100, RPC +71) to fill at cash-patient
prices within three years, or the SSO to restore the high-cost inpatient rate; either takes
earnings to ฿1.1–1.2bn and the stock to ฿2+. To call it expensive you need occupancy to keep
falling into a capacity increase, which is what 2Q26 looked like. The middle — flat earnings, a
฿0.07 dividend and a slow bleed of net cash into construction — is what is priced.

## Operating picture — a price/mix business with a government floor

**Volumes are falling, faster than at BH.** Inpatient admissions 95,332 in 2025 (+0.8%) but
−10.3% in 1H26 and −13.4% in 2Q26; bed-days 225,504 (2023) → 228,141 → 219,578 (2025), occupancy
on the 938-bed base 64.1% in 2025, 57.7% in 1Q26, 53.7% in 2Q26. Outpatient visits −3.4% in 1H26;
exam-room utilisation ~60%. There is no capacity constraint anywhere, and the group is about to add
75% to it.

**Revenue is being held up by intensity and by government referrals.** General (cash, insurance,
corporate) outpatient revenue +8% in 2025 and +8% in 1H26 on falling visits — revenue per visit
฿2,078 (2023) → ฿2,250 (2025). General inpatient revenue flat in 2025 and −8% in 1H26 (−14% in
2Q26): the co-payment health-insurance policies sold since March 2025 make patients defer elective
admissions, and GLP-1 weight-loss injections have taken the bariatric-surgery line that was a
2023–24 growth driver (SSO gastric-sleeve revenue "fell to almost zero" in 2025). "Other
government" — heart, cancer and stroke referrals from public hospitals under the universal-coverage
scheme, plus three heart-centre management contracts — grew +22% in 2025 and +31% in 1H26 to 5–6%
of revenue. That is the cleanest growth line in the group and it is a public-budget line.

**Social Security is 28% of revenue and it is a tariff, not a market.** Registrants 528,111 (2023)
→ 542,661 → 564,836 (2025) → 577,527 (2Q26), against a quota of 634,000; capitation ฿1,808 per
head since May-2023, unchanged. But SSO revenue per registrant fell ฿4,660 (2023) → ฿4,591 →
฿4,129 (2025), −11% in two years, because the SSO cut the high-cost (adjusted RW ≥ 2) rate in
mid-2024 and tightened pre-authorisation, and CHG wrote down accrued 2023 high-cost income (฿53M)
and 2025 chronic-disease income (~฿103M). SSO revenue rose +10% in 2Q26 on the low base. The SSO
subcommittee is reviewing capitation, chronic-disease and high-cost rates with a resolution
expected 24-Oct-2026 (broker-sourced date); a 5% rate change is worth ~8% of earnings. The
board's April-2026 resolution 5/2569 also introduced pre-payment audits of high-cost claims with
payment withheld up to three months, and ordered refunds of audited overpayments across 290
contracted hospitals. **This is a regulator that has cut twice in two years; the October review is
the single largest near-term swing factor.**

**Government receivables are the CHG version of BH's Gulf receivables, smaller and structural.**
Accrued medical-treatment income from the SSO ฿691M (Dec-24) → ฿646M (Dec-25) → ฿761M (Jun-26);
from the NHSO ฿92M → 83 → 127M; plus ฿203M of trade receivables from government contracts. About
฿1.09bn of government paper at Jun-26, ~4.6 months of government revenue, against ฿39M of expected
credit-loss allowance. The 2024 and 2025 write-downs show the accrual is estimated, not
contractual. Separately, ฿278M of COVID-era accrued income was written off in 2025 against a
matching liability (the P&L effect is not spelled out in the MD&A). **Rule: read the accrued-income
note every half-year; a third consecutive write-down would say the SSO accrual is being booked
too aggressively.**

**Margins are stable and low-quality relative to BH.** Gross margin 27.2% (2023) → 26.3% → 28.4%
(2025) → 27.5% (1H26); company EBITDA margin 21.0% → 20.9% → 20.6% (1H26), 19.7% in 2Q26. The 2025
improvement came from cost control (Pattaya exit, solar, lower drug costs), not pricing. 2Q26 cost
of operations +3.3% on +2.0% revenue.

**International is 4–5% of revenue and concentrated at CH3.** Middle East 49% of the international
centre's 2Q26 patients, Myanmar 44%, Cambodia 4% (the border dispute took Cambodia down from 15% in
2025). 2Q26 inpatient was hit by Middle East patients staying away during the US–Iran tension.
Small enough not to drive the value; large enough to explain a bad quarter.

**The expansion is the story and the risk.** Rayong: 200-bed greenfield, ฿1.5bn, opening 2027
(59 beds in the first phase, per the deck; brokers say 2H27–4Q27), targeting cash, corporate and
insured patients on the eastern seaboard. CH3 buildings 5–6: +100 beds, ฿500M, 2027. RPC +71 beds
(2H26). CH9 +100 and Mae Sot +59 "to be confirmed". Committed capital ฿418M (Dec-25) → ฿1,088M
(Jun-26). The precedent is Mae Sot (opened Jun-2023): ฿87M loss in year one on ฿180M revenue, ฿27M
in year three; brokers model break-even in 2028. Rayong is eight times Mae Sot's size. Expect
฿100–150M/yr of pretax start-up drag in 2027–28, 10–15% of a year's profit, plus the D&A step-up.
Free cash flow after growth capex will run ~฿0.4–0.6bn in 2026–27 against a ฿770M dividend, so
the ฿0.8bn of net cash becomes modest net debt by end-2027. Management's own line — D/E 0.24×
leaves "substantial capacity" — is true, and it also says the dividend will be partly borrowed.

## Capital return

Policy ≥50% of consolidated profit; actual ฿0.07/sh (฿770M) in each of FY23, FY24 and FY25, i.e.
payout rose from 74% to 83% as profit fell. The FY26 interim was held at ฿0.02. The signal is a
board that treats ฿770M as a floor, not a ratio — good for the income case, but it leaves no room
for a step-up while the capex cycle runs, and it means the dividend does not grow until 2028 at
the earliest. Hold-return arithmetic at a frozen multiple: yield 4.4% + growth 2–3% ≈ **6.5–7.5%/yr
in THB**, before the gross-up credit. No buyback history; loan covenants require the family to
maintain its stake, so no change of control either.

## Dividend-tax gross-up: where CHG sits against TLI, BH and PTT

**Verdict: CHG's dividend is as credit-clean as TLI's and BH's — the AGM resolution reports for
2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 (FY22–FY25 finals) and the Aug-2024 and Aug-2026 interim notices all
state the dividend is paid "from the profit that is subject to the corporate income tax at the
rate of 20 percent"; the Aug-2023 and Aug-2025 interim SET forms carry no source line but sit
between years that do, and the tax note shows no BOI holiday (effective rate 20.6%). But at a 4.4% cash yield it
ranks fourth: behind TLI, behind BH if BH's ~฿9 run-rate holds, and marginally behind PTT at a 10%
marginal rate.**

Same mechanics as in the BH note (Section 47 bis: credit = 20/80 of the cash dividend on
20%-taxed profit; election is all-or-nothing; break-even marginal rate 28% for a credit

## Sensitivity map — what moves the value

- **Long-run growth.** Each +0.5% of perpetual growth adds ~8% to fair value (฿1.63 → ฿1.76 →
  ฿1.91 at 2.5 / 3 / 3.5%, Ke 9%). The evidence for ≥3% is the +75% bed pipeline, +8%
  outpatient intensity and +31% government referrals; the evidence against is three years of
  falling profit and 54% occupancy.
- **Cost of equity.** ±0.5% moves value ∓8% (฿1.76 → ฿1.63 at Ke 9.5%, g 3%). Half the
  argument between this note's "fair" and the sell side's ฿1.80–2.00 targets is their 7.5–8%
  discount rates (labelled: InnovestX WACC 7.5%, KGI 7.9%, ttb 9.3%).
- **SSO rates (Oct-2026 review).** ±5% on SSO reimbursement ≈ ±8% of earnings (broker
  estimate, consistent with SSO at 28% of revenue and ~35% incremental margin). Two cuts in
  2024–25 are the base rate; a restoration is the upside tail.
- **Occupancy into the expansion.** Every 1 point of occupancy on 938 beds ≈ 3,400 bed-days ≈
  ฿130M revenue ≈ ฿45M EBITDA ≈ 4% of earnings. 2Q26 was 7.6 points below 2Q25.
- **Start-up losses 2027–28.** ฿100–150M/yr pretax ≈ 10–15% of earnings; a valuation
  non-event if Rayong ramps like CH304/RPC did (profitable within ~3 years), a thesis-breaker
  if it ramps like Mae Sot.
- **Insurance co-payment and GLP-1.** General inpatient revenue −8% in 1H26 is the sum of the
  two; neither reverses on its own. Management's answer is packaged value-based care.

Candidate for the income sleeve, not for the growth sleeve. Nothing argues for urgency: the price

## Terms (for cold reads)

- **SSO / capitation:** the Social Security Office pays a fixed ฿1,808 per registered worker per
  year regardless of use, plus statistics-based top-ups for chronic disease and high-cost
  inpatient cases (adjusted relative weight, "RW"). The registrant chooses the hospital; the
  hospital bears the cost risk.
- **Accrued medical-treatment income:** revenue booked for SSO/NHSO services before the payer
  has confirmed it; an estimate that the payer can cut, as it did in 2024 and 2025.
- **NHSO / UCS:** the universal-coverage ("30-baht") scheme; CHG is a referral provider for
  heart, cancer and stroke cases, not a primary provider.
- **General patients:** cash, private-insurance and corporate-contract patients (67% of revenue).
- **Occupancy:** inpatient bed-days ÷ (average beds × days).
- **ROIC / implied growth:** as in the BH note — after-tax operating profit ÷ (equity −
  net cash); and the perpetual growth rate at which FCF ÷ (Ke − g) + net cash equals the market
  cap.
