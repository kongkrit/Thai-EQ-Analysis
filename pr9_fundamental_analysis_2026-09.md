## Verdict

**Fair to modestly cheap — the only one of the five Thai hospital stocks whose growth record
exceeds what its price discounts.** At ฿19.30 PR9 discounts ~2.5–3% perpetual free-cash-flow
growth (on a normalised ฿0.72bn of FCF; the FY25 print of ฿0.46bn was depressed by an investment
year) at an 8.5–9% cost of equity, from a single hospital that has grown revenue at 15%/yr for five
years (฿2.6bn in 2020 → ฿5.3bn in 2025, +13.9% in 2025), earns ~25% on invested capital, carries
฿2.8bn of net cash (฿3.55/sh, 18% of the price) and has never borrowed. Fair band ฿17.5–24;
central ฿19–22 (3–4% growth, Ke 8.5–9%). The price is at the bottom of the central range.

Why it is not simply cheap: 2026 is a step-down year (1H26 revenue +3.4%, net profit −3.6% on a
14% rise in depreciation and a 42% fall in interest income), the growth of 2025 was one contract
(Qatar's Al Koot insurer took Arab-League revenue +748% and contract-payer revenue +142% to 15%
of the total), the hospital is one building on one site with 204 beds at 69% occupancy and 77%
in intensive care, and 39% of the equity is held by Khunying Potjaman Damapong and the Shinawatra
children. The discount is concentration — of site, of payer, and of owner — and it is not going
away. What it buys you is the cheapest operating business in the group (9.6× EBITDA on net cash)
with the best recent numbers.

## What the price implies

Strip ฿2.8bn of net cash and the hospital is priced at ฿12.4bn, 9.6× EBITDA and ~16× after-tax
operating profit. At 8.5% that is fair if normalised free cash flow compounds at ~2.7% forever.
The record: revenue ฿4.25bn (2023) → 4.69 → 5.34bn (2025); net profit ฿558M → 713 → 823M; gross
margin 32.9% → 34.3% → 36.6%; EBITDA margin 22.5% → 23.8% → 23.9%. Three-year earnings CAGR ~21%.
1H26: revenue +3.4%, EBITDA +1.6%, net profit −3.6%, margin 23.5%. Management guides FY26 revenue
"mid-single digit" with EBITDA margin ≥23% and capex under ฿650M; labelled consensus (14 analysts)
has FY26 EPS ฿1.08 (+3%) and a ฿23 average target.

To call ฿19.30 expensive you need 2025 to have been a one-off — the Qatar contract not renewed,
Myanmar (record quarter in 2Q26) reversing, and the 2026 depreciation step-up permanent. To call it
cheap you need only that the business keeps doing what it did in 2022–25 at half the rate. The
asymmetry favours the second reading, which is why the note says modestly cheap rather than fair.

## Operating picture — a single hospital growing by case mix and by Qatar

**Capacity is the constraint, and it is being lifted inside the existing buildings.** 204
registered beds (177 general, 24 ICU/CCU, 3 NICU), unchanged since 2021; occupancy 60% (2023–24)
→ 69% (2025) → 67–70% in 1H26; intensive-care occupancy 77% in 2Q26. Inpatient days 44,904
(2024) → 51,661 (2025, +15%) → flat in 1H26. Plans: +20 general and +6 ICU beds to reach ~230 in
2026 (press-sourced), critical-care beds 24 → 31 in 4Q26 (One Report timeline), potential 300 beds
within buildings A and B (241 + 59) without new construction. Outpatient: 1,581 visits/day in 2025
→ 1,481 in 1H26 (−4 to −6%), rooms 41–45% utilised. **The whole 2026 top line is intensity:**
inpatient revenue per day +10% in 2025 and +6% in 1H26 (+10.6% in 2Q26); outpatient revenue per
visit +3% and +5%. Revenue per inpatient day ~฿46,700 (2025), above BDMS's ~฿34,500 and below
BH's ~฿100,000 — PR9 is priced as a mid-premium Bangkok hospital and bills like one.

**International went from 14% to 27% of revenue in two years.** ฿576M (2023) → ฿786M (2024, +36%)
→ ฿1,360M (2025, +73%) → ฿711M in 1H26 (+10%; 29% of 2Q26 revenue, the second-highest quarter on
record). The driver is a B2B arrangement with Al Koot, Qatar's insurer for airline and oil-company
employees, booked as contract-payer revenue: ฿284M (2023) → 334 → 808M (2025, +142%, 15% of
hospital revenue). Arab-League revenue +748% in 2025, +7.6% in 2Q26 as the regional conflict bit;
CLMV +31.5% in 2Q26 on Myanmar (a Mandalay referral office opened in 1H26); China −17%. Middle
East is roughly a third of foreign patients. Kuwait is an aspiration (management cites rising
self-pay enquiries); Cambodia is immaterial. **Concentration rule: one insurer contract is ~15% of
revenue and most of the 2025 growth; its renewal terms are not disclosed.**

**Payer mix is entirely private — no Social Security, no universal-coverage capitation.**
Self-pay 61%, insurance 24%, contract 15% (2025); 58/27/15 in 2Q26 with self-pay −4% and insurance
+20%. The co-payment rule is, per management, "a positive opportunity" because PR9 sits below
BH/BDMS on price; 1H26 insurance revenue +7.8% supports that. No government tariff means no SSO
review risk and no accrued-income estimation risk; the receivables book is small (฿644M gross,
DSO 25–32 days) and well provided (฿74M allowance against ฿64M more than a year old).

**Franchise.** Kidney Disease and Transplantation Institute (the private hospital with the most
kidney transplants in Thailand, >1,461 cumulative; first Kazakh and Bangladeshi transplant patients
in 2025; premium haemodialysis and peritoneal-dialysis clinics opened 1H26), Cardiovascular
Institute (31 specialists; TAVI, EP, CABG), neuroscience, robotic orthopaedics, da Vinci Xi
robotic surgery (2026), IVF, LASIK, gender-health surgery, W9 Wellness. Five declared centres of
excellence; JCI since 2010; ranked ninth in Thailand by Newsweek 2025.

**Costs.** Physicians' fees +13% and salaries +11% in 2025 on +14% revenue; SG&A 17.8% → 19.3% of
revenue on international marketing (cut back to 20.0% in 2Q26 with selling expenses at 4.3%);
D&A +14% in 1H26 and +18% in 2Q26 from the new operating theatres, ward 12A, the dialysis units,
da Vinci and a new HIS/ERP — capex ฿249M (2024) → ฿530M (2025) → ฿251M (1H26). Finance income
฿51M (2025) → ฿16M (1H26) as rates fell on ฿2.8bn of deposits and bonds. The 2026 profit dip is
D&A and interest income, not the hospital.

## Capital return

Policy ≥40% of profit; actual 40–48% since 2021 (฿0.14 → 0.29 → 0.30 → 0.40 → 0.50); FY26 interim
฿0.20, flat on last year's. Cash paid ฿351M in each of 2024 and 2025 against ฿0.5–0.8bn of free
cash flow, so net cash keeps growing (฿2.62bn → 2.77 → 2.79bn) and the return on equity is being
dragged to 14.5% by ฿2.8bn earning ~2%. The board has shown no appetite for a special or a
buyback; a move to a 60–70% payout (฿0.65–0.75) would lift the yield to 3.4–3.9% and cost
nothing. Hold-return arithmetic at a frozen multiple: yield 2.6% + growth 4–5% ≈ **6.5–7.5%/yr in
THB**, with the cash pile as unpriced optionality.

## Sensitivity map — what moves the value

- **Long-run growth.** Each +0.5% adds ~8% (฿20.2 → ฿21.9 → ฿23.9 at 3 / 3.5 / 4%, Ke 8.5%). The
  evidence for ≥3.5%: five-year record, capacity headroom to 300 beds, international at 27% and
  rising, no tariff exposure. Against: 1H26 +3.4%, one-contract dependence, a full ICU.
- **The FCF anchor.** Using reported FY25 FCF (฿458M) instead of normalised (฿720M) cuts fair
  value ~30% (฿20.2 → ฿14.1). The difference is whether ฿530M of 2025 capex recurs; guidance
  (<฿650M in 2026, essentials only) says it does, for one more year.
- **Cost of equity.** ±0.5% ≈ ∓8%.
- **Qatar / contract payer.** ฿808M of revenue; a 30% cut ≈ ฿240M revenue ≈ ฿100M EBITDA ≈ 10% of
  earnings. Renewal terms undisclosed. Kuwait's return would be the mirror-image upside.
- **Occupancy.** 1 point on 204 beds ≈ 745 bed-days ≈ ฿35M revenue ≈ 1.5% of earnings; the ICU
  at 77% is the binding constraint until the 4Q26 expansion.
- **Depreciation and interest income.** 2026 D&A ~฿350M (+12%) and finance income ~฿30M (−40%)
  together take ~฿55M off pretax profit ≈ 5%; both normalise from 2027.
- **Payout.** A move to 65% payout adds ~1 point of yield at no cost to the balance sheet.

politics, and the register shows it.

## Relation to the register

Candidate for the growth sleeve — the only one of the five where the operating record argues for
buying rather than waiting. Not urgent: 2026 is a known step-down year and the stock has already
priced it; the 3Q26 print (13-Nov-2026) is the first quarter with the ICU expansion approaching and
the Middle East recovery in the base, and management and the sell side both call it the year's
strongest quarter. A print in the ฿17s with international share still ≥26% is a 10%+ margin of
safety to the central value with the cash pile as a floor. Re-check at FY26 results (Feb-2027): the
contract-payer line (Qatar renewal), inpatient days on the enlarged bed base, and whether the
board moves the payout.

## Terms (for cold reads)

- **Contract payer:** corporate or insurer contracts (here principally Al Koot, Qatar) that pay
  PR9 directly for their members' treatment; distinct from Thai private insurance.
- **Arab League / CLMV:** PR9's reporting groups for international revenue — Gulf and other Arab
  states; Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Vietnam.
- **Normalised FCF:** operating cash flow less an estimate of the capex needed to maintain the
  hospital (~D&A), as opposed to the reported figure that includes expansion spending.
- **Revenue per inpatient day:** inpatient revenue ÷ inpatient days; the intensity measure that
  has carried the 2025–26 top line.
- **ROIC / implied growth:** as in the BH note; invested capital = equity − net cash, which is
  small here because the land is carried at 1992 cost.
