## Verdict

**Modestly rich. At ฿11.40 BCH is the most expensive of the five Thai hospital stocks on earnings
(23.4× trailing) with the lowest return on capital (~10.6% ROIC, 10% ROE), three flat years
(revenue ฿11.8 / 11.8 / 12.0bn; net profit ฿1,406 / 1,282 / 1,316M; 1H26 −14%), and a payer mix
drifting toward the Social Security tariff (37.5% of revenue and rising).** The price discounts
3–3.5% perpetual free-cash-flow growth; fair band ฿9–12.5, central ฿10–11.5 (3–3.5% growth, 9%
cost of equity). What the market is paying for is the largest bed pipeline in the sector relative
to size — 2,323 licensed beds to ~3,450 by 2029 (+49%), two 270-bed greenfields, a 100-bed
acquisition and three building extensions, ~฿6–7bn of capex — and management's JUMP+ target of
฿14.5bn revenue in 2028 (+6.5%/yr from 2025). None of it earns before 2028, and the group has
already gone from ฿0.8bn net cash to net debt in the first half of 2026 while paying a special
dividend.

What is real: the largest Social Security operator in the country (1.02M registrants), 15
hospitals with real scale in the Bangkok periphery and the eastern seaboard, an A+ credit rating,
half-owned by a founder who has run it for four decades. What is not yet real is the growth.

## What the price implies

Add ฿0.1bn of net debt and ฿1.0bn of minorities to the ฿28.4bn market cap and the operating
business is priced at ฿29.5bn, 10.7× EBITDA and ~21× after-tax operating profit. At a 9% cost of
equity that is fair only if free cash flow compounds at ~3.6% forever from the FY25 level. The
record since the COVID windfall ended: EBITDA ฿2,903M (2023) → 2,709 → 2,881 → TTM 2,763; net
profit ฿1,406M → 1,282 → 1,316 → TTM 1,218; 1H26 EBITDA −8%, net profit −14%. Some of the 1H26
weakness is weather (respiratory cases −40% nationally), war (Middle East revenue −24% in March
before rebounding +43% in 2Q26) and the Cambodian border (Cambodian revenue −87%). Management calls
2Q26 the trough and brokers agree. Even granting that, the pre-trough level was flat.

To call ฿11.40 cheap you need the JUMP+ plan to deliver: ฿14.5bn revenue in 2028 at a 24% margin
is ฿3.5bn EBITDA, and at 10.5× less ~฿3bn of net debt that is ~฿13.5/sh in 2028 — a 6%/yr return
including dividends from here. To call it expensive you need the two greenfields to ramp like KH
Prachinburi and KIH Aranyaprathet did (both still EBITDA-negative years after opening) while SSO
rates get cut again. The price sits nearer the first case than the record justifies.

## Operating picture — a Social Security utility with a private-patient problem

**Cash patients are shrinking; the tariff patients are growing.** Hospital revenue by payer,
1H26 vs 1H25: general outpatient ฿2,037M (−5.8%), general inpatient ฿1,643M (−0.2%), Social
Security ฿2,206M (+4.3%). SSO was 33% of revenue in 2024, 35% in 2025, 37.5% in 1H26. Self-pay
revenue fell 4.4% in 2025 and ~11.5% in 2Q26; corporate contracts (+14% in 2025, embassy contracts
+194% in 2Q26) and private insurance (+3.6%) are what is holding the general segment up. Patient
volumes rose (+3.6% in 1H26) because SSO outpatient utilisation is rising — 3.05 → 3.44 visits per
registrant per year — which under capitation is a cost, not revenue.

**SSO economics.** 1,027,813 registrants on average in 2025 (+0.6%), quota raised to 1.99M for
2026 but registration rate falling (55% → 51%). Capitation ฿1,808/head since May-2023 (~45% of SSO
revenue), high-cost inpatient reimbursement (adjusted RW > 2, ~19%), 26 chronic diseases (~14%),
radiation/other outpatient (~11%), dental (~7%). The SSO cut the high-cost rate in 2024 (−฿245M
revenue, −฿191M net profit, booked in 2Q24 and 4Q24) and BCH reversed a further ฿53M in 4Q25 for
claim-data discrepancies. SSO revenue per head ฿3,807 (2024) → ฿4,037 (2025) → ฿4,515 annualised
2Q26, helped each year by a ~฿70–77M chronic-disease surplus booked in the second quarter. The
same subcommittee review that matters for CHG (rates for capitation, chronic and high-cost care;
resolution expected Oct-2026) is a ~37%-of-revenue swing factor here; the Section 33 wage-ceiling
increase from Jan-2026 gives the fund more money to distribute. **The accrued SSO receivable rose
from ฿911M (Dec-25) to ฿1,199M (Jun-26), a 32% jump in six months on revenue growth of 4%, with
฿2.5M of allowance against it; the auditor flagged it as a key audit matter.** That is the number to
read first in every set of statements.

**International is 12–13% of revenue and volatile.** ฿1,710M in 2024 → ฿1,507M in 2025 (−12%;
+6% ex-Cambodia). Kuwait government referrals have been suspended since 2024 (World Medical
Hospital +0.2% in 2025, +8.4% ex-Kuwait); ฿246M of old Kuwaiti receivables were finally collected
via the embassy in 2025, which is why the 181–365-day bucket collapsed from ฿251M to ฿38M. The
replacement is Qatar and UAE embassy contracts (Middle East +43% in 2Q26, inpatient revenue per
bill ฿1.6M vs ฿0.8M). Cambodia was the CLMV anchor and is gone (−87%); Laos (Kasemrad
International Vientiane, 110 beds) is finally profitable (EBITDA ฿66M in 2025) but sits on ฿886M of
unrecognised Lao tax losses and 9.1% kip borrowings.

**Margins are mid-table and drifting.** Gross margin 30.8% (2023) → 27.3% → 27.9% → 27.5% (1H26);
EBITDA margin 24.5% → 22.9% → 24.0% → 23.5% (1H26), 22.0% in 1Q26. Doctor fees (20% of revenue,
+1.4%) and personnel (20.8%, +3.4%) grew faster than revenue in 2025; D&A (9% of revenue) is
already rising ahead of the new hospitals. Cost wins in 2025 came from referral cost (−6.7%),
utilities (solar, −8%) and rent (−21%).

**The expansion.** Kasemrad Rayong 270 beds, ฿1.48bn, opening 4Q27; Kasemrad Suvarnabhumi 270
beds, ฿1.65bn, 1Q28; World Medical Pattaya 150–200 beds, ฿1.85bn, 2029; Rattanatibeth 119 → 220
beds by 2029; Ramkhamhaeng +120 beds, 2028; Mae Sai 30 → 38 beds, Sep-2026; Rajavej Ubon
Ratchathani hospital assets bought for ≤฿490M (100 beds, to reopen as Kasemrad International
Ubon). Contracted commitments ฿2.3bn at Jun-26. Against CFO of ~฿2.7bn/yr, ฿1.5–2bn/yr of capex
and ฿1.1–1.9bn/yr of dividends, net debt goes to ~฿3–4bn by 2028 (≈1.2× EBITDA; covenants 3.25×;
TRIS assumes 0.5–1.0×). The balance sheet can carry it. The P&L will show it first as start-up
losses: BCH's own subscale hospitals already lose ~฿150M/yr at the EBITDA line (Prachinburi −฿32M,
Aranyaprathet −฿8M, Sothon Vejchakit −฿71M net, Navanakorn −฿33M net), and two 270-bed greenfields
will each add ฿150–250M of pretax drag in year one.

## Capital return

Policy ≥40% of separate-company profit; actual FY25 ฿0.45 = 85% of consolidated profit (76% of
separate). The FY26 interim of ฿0.35 (฿0.15 ordinary + ฿0.20 special, ฿873M) was declared in the
same month as a ฿490M acquisition and a quarter in which net cash turned to net debt; it equals
133% of first-half separate EPS. Read it as the family's cash-out ahead of a leverage cycle, not
as a payout-policy signal. Regular run-rate ~฿0.45–0.50 → 3.9–4.4% yield; hold-return arithmetic
at a frozen multiple: yield ~4% + growth ~3% ≈ **7%/yr in THB**, but the growth half is the
unproven half. No buyback; no treasury shares; no convertibles.

## Sensitivity map — what moves the value

- **Long-run growth.** Each +0.5% adds ~9% to value (฿10.3 → ฿11.3 → ฿12.4 at 3 / 3.5 / 4%,
  Ke 9%). The +49% bed pipeline is the whole case for ≥3.5%; three flat years and a shrinking
  cash-patient base are the case against.
- **Cost of equity.** ±0.5% ≈ ∓9%.
- **SSO rates (Oct-2026 review).** At 37.5% of revenue and ~35% incremental margin, a 5% rate
  change ≈ ±10% of earnings; the 2024 cut alone was −15% of that year's profit.
- **The SSO accrual.** ฿1.2bn accrued against ฿2.5M of allowance; a 10% haircut = ฿120M ≈ 10% of
  a year's profit.
- **Start-up losses 2027–29.** Two 270-bed greenfields plus Ubon and Pattaya: ฿300–500M/yr pretax
  at peak ≈ 20–30% of current earnings. The 2028 JUMP+ revenue target assumes they ramp fast.
- **Middle East and Cambodia.** Together ~5–6% of revenue, both externally switched (Kuwait
  policy, border closure). Recovery is upside; neither is in the base case.
- **Kip and Laos.** ฿240M of kip borrowings at 9.1%, ฿886M of unusable tax losses, hyperinflation
  accounting only just discontinued; a ฿660M impairment was already taken through equity in 2024.

## Ownership and governance

## Relation to the register

Not a candidate at this price. The stock is priced for a recovery plus an expansion, and the
evidence for either is one month of July data and a set of construction slabs. Two things would
change the view: an SSO rate restoration in October 2026 (re-rates the whole SSO book, BCH most of
all), or a print below ฿9 (where the FY25 FCF yield is 7% and the pipeline comes free). Re-check
at the 3Q26 results (Nov-2026) for the accrued-SSO line and the 2H26 recovery, and at the FY26
dividend notice for the source split.

## Terms (for cold reads)

- **SSO capitation / adjusted RW:** see the CHG note. The SSO pays a fixed ฿1,808 per registrant
  per year plus top-ups for chronic and high-cost inpatient cases weighted by relative weight
  (RW); "RW > 2" is the high-cost tier whose rate the SSO cut in 2024.
- **Accrued income – SSO:** revenue booked for SSO services before the SSO has confirmed the
  amount; a management estimate the payer can later reduce.
- **Registration rate:** registrants ÷ quota — how full BCH's SSO allocation is.
- **General patients:** self-pay, private-insurance and corporate/embassy-contract patients.
- **JUMP+:** the March-2026 strategic plan targeting ฿14.5bn+ revenue and 20 hospitals by 2028.
- **ROIC / implied growth:** as in the BH note.
